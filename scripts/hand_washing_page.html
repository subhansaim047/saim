    Whether the aim is to stop a global pandemic in communities or to prevent the spread of germs in a food establishment, hand washing is one of the best ways to get the job done. Foodborne pathogens such as bacteria and viruses that can cause foodborne illnesses and other health threats can easily be spread through contact.

    A food handler can unknowingly hold a contaminated object and spread the contaminant all over other areas.

    4. The Centers for Disease Control stated that proper hand washing with soapy and warm running water could significantly reduce the occurrence of deaths from gastrointestinal diseases by 50%.

    What is a hand washing steps poster?

    A hand washing poster is a visual safety sign containing a step-by-step process to properly wash your hands. Although a very common hygiene action, you'd be surprised at how easily you could get it wrong.

    Correct hand washing procedures must be thorough as part of your everyday healthy habits and bathroom rules. It would not be enough just to rinse your hands with water and then dry it to remove harmful germs such as bacteria and viruses.

    A hand hygiene poster is a step guide to show which areas need to be covered properly when washing.

    Hand washing is part of the many important food safety practices in the food industry. It is one of the most effective preventive measures against the spread of food poisoning due to cross-contamination with contaminated hands.

    A hand washing poster with guidance highlights essential information, including:

    • Wash hands for at least 20 seconds to ensure that all pathogens on your hand are killed. You can use the "Happy Birthday" song to reference how long it takes to wash your hands.
    • Use clean, running tap water with liquid soap to prevent recontamination of hands.
    • Dry hands after washing. Wet hands are more likely to become contaminated during food operations.

    Why is cleaning hands important for food businesses?

    Proper hand washing as part of control programmes can save lives. Similar to the case of the COVID-19 pandemic, pathogens such as viruses and bacteria can easily be transmitted through human contact with foods and food contact surfaces.

    Food handlers must always know when and how to properly wash their hands to keep their food operations safe at all times and prevent the spread of infectious diseases.

    Hand hygiene is crucial for several reasons:

    1. Prevents spreading foodborne illnesses. Contaminated hands can be a significant source of foodborne pathogens. Food handlers who do not practice proper hand hygiene may transfer harmful bacteria, viruses, or other contaminants to the food they handle, posing a serious risk to consumer safety. 
    2. Promotes overall health. Promoting good hand hygiene practices among employees is not only important for food safety but also for their own health and well-being. Regular handwashing helps protect employees from contracting and spreading illnesses in the workplace, promoting productivity and maintaining a healthy workforce.
    3. Ensures regulatory compliance. Food safety regulations and standards often require food businesses to maintain strict hand hygiene practices. Compliance with these regulations is not only necessary to meet legal obligations but also demonstrates a commitment to food safety.
    4. Maintains customer trust. Maintaining high standards of hygiene, including proper hand washing, is crucial for preserving reputation and building customer trust. Customers prefer to dine or buy from food businesses with employees who evidently practice proper hand hygiene.

    It is a fundamental practice that should be prioritized to uphold the highest standards of hygiene and safety in the food industry. Food business owners can promote proper hand hygiene by providing the necessary tools and visual aids used for hand washing.

    What are the proper hand washing steps?

    Proper hand washing involves a set of steps that must be performed in proper order. The effectiveness of the hand washing process depends if the steps are followed correctly. 

    The World Health Organization (WHO) has established 7 effective hand washing steps similar to health care facility level to prevent any spread of infection:

    1. Wet your hands and cover your hands in soap
    2. Rub right and left palms together
    3. Rub the back of hands while interlacing fingers
    4. Rub hands while interlacing fingers
    5. Rub the back of the fingers
    6. Rub thumbs
    7. Rub the tips of fingers

    These simple steps for proper hand washing technique are basic, and steps 2 to 7 can be interchanged as everything is done.

    Additional essential steps, such as rinsing afterwards and drying hands with a dry, disposable paper towel, can also be added to your hand washing awareness poster. Hand drying is an important part of the handwashing process because it reduces the risk of pathogens clinging to the sanitized hands.

    What is the minimum amount of time required to complete the handwashing process?

    Researchers have proven that hand washing is most effective when done for a minimum time of 20 seconds.  

    This duration allows for thorough cleaning and removal of germs from the hands. It is important to note that the 20 seconds should be spent actively washing the hands, including all surfaces such as palms, back of hands, between fingers, and around nails, using soap and water.

    Rushing through the handwashing process may compromise its effectiveness in removing pathogens effectively.

    What are the materials used in hand washing?

    The most important material in regular hand sanitising for proper hygiene is a source of clean and warm water. Your tap water must be free from contaminants and have passed the minimum requirements to be considered potable.

    Additionally, the following hygiene materials must be present to accomplish basic hand washing techniques:

    • Anti-bacterial hand wash, bar soap, or detergent
    • Paper towels
    • Alcohol or alcohol-based hand sanitisers (optional)
    • Fingernail brush (optional)

    It's worth noting that in situations where water and soap are accessible, traditional handwashing with soap and water is generally preferred over hand sanitisers. Hand sanitisers are recommended as a convenient option when soap and water are not available, but they may not effectively remove certain types of contaminants, such as chemicals or heavy soiling.

     

    What is the most important aspect of hand washing?

    Perhaps the most important factor in hand washing is the length of time you spend on the process. This helps ensure that all pathogens are soaked with soap to prevent the spread of bacteria.

    During this time, the food handler must actively perform the proper steps to optimize the hand washing process. 

    Who needs a hand washing poster?

    All food establishments, manufacturing, retail shops, groceries, and other food-related businesses would benefit from a simple poster for hand washing. Routine hand hygiene is a basic food hygiene practice that must be practised by all food handlers for infection prevention and control.

    Basic homeowners, healthcare workers, and schools can even use a hand washing poster as an approach to safety. Practically, any industry with hygiene facilities can use a hand washing poster to help keep their hands clean.

    Health authorities from food agencies suggest washing hands with plain soap before and after food preparation and on other occasions, such as the following, for effective infection prevention:

    • After going to the bathroom.
    • In between handling raw foods and ready-to-eat products.
    • After throwing garbage.
    • After handling live animals.
    • When hands are soiled.

    Frequent and effective hand hygiene significantly reduces the levels of bacteria and other infectious germs on food handlers' hands.

    Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

    What is the second stage of handwashing?

    The second stage of handwashing is lathering the hands with soap. This step helps to remove dirt, germs, and oils from the skin by creating a soapy lather.

    What is the correct first step in handwashing?

    The correct first step in handwashing is wetting the hands with clean, running water. Wetting the hands before applying soap helps to initiate the process of removing dirt and germs. It also activates the soap and allows it to reach all areas of the hands. 

    How many steps are in hand washing?

    Handwashing typically consists of seven main steps: wetting the hands, applying soap, scrubbing for at least 20 seconds, rubbing the soap in between fingers, rubbing the back of the hand, and rinsing the hands thoroughly with clean water.
